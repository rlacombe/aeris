STUDY: Wearable Cardiac Biomarkers and Ambient Air Quality in Southern California
PI: [Demo — Synthetic Data]
IRB: Approved (exempt — synthetic data for demonstration)

OBJECTIVE:
Examine the association between residential PM2.5 exposure and
cardiac autonomic function — measured via wearable-derived heart
rate variability (HRV) and resting heart rate (RHR) — in adults
across a pollution gradient in Southern California.

MOTIVATION:
PM2.5 has been linked to autonomic dysfunction in controlled
exposure studies. HRV (RMSSD) decreases and RHR increases with
pollution exposure, reflecting sympatho-vagal imbalance. Wearable
devices now make it feasible to capture these biomarkers passively
at population scale. Southern California offers a natural pollution
gradient from clean coastal areas to heavily impacted inland and
port-adjacent communities.

COHORT:
- 15 adults aged 27-55 wearing consumer activity trackers
- Recruited from 8 ZIP codes spanning coastal LA (90401),
  Westside (90034), Downtown (90012), Lincoln Heights (90031),
  Pasadena (91101), Long Beach port area (90802, 90744),
  and the Inland Empire (91762, 92507)
- Observation period: January 15 – March 15, 2024

OUTCOMES:
- Primary: Mean HRV (RMSSD, ms) over observation period
- Secondary: Mean resting heart rate (bpm)

COVARIATES:
- Age, sex, BMI, smoking status
- Physical activity (mean daily steps)
- Sleep duration (hours/night)

EXPOSURE OF INTEREST:
- Ambient PM2.5 concentration at residential ZIP code
- Need to integrate monitoring network data for study period

ANALYSIS PLAN:
- Linear regression: HRV ~ PM2.5 + age + sex + bmi + smoker + activity + sleep
- Same for RHR
- Assess effect modification by smoking status
- Sensitivity: try NO2 as alternative exposure
